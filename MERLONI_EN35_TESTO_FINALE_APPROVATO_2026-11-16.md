# Aristide Merloni — Enterprise and the Fate of Places

*Work, industry and the contradictions of development in inland Marche.*

**Cerchi d'inchiostro · Guide 35 · Twelfth triad: State, Enterprise and Community**

By Alessandro Gentili · Final International editorial source approved 16 November 2026 · Adapted from the approved Italian revised source of 16 November 2026

## Introduction — Work and place

In 1930 Aristide Merloni returned to Albacina, a village in the Fabriano district of inland Marche, after years of industrial apprenticeship in Piedmont. He knew how weighing instruments were made, had worked with metal and machinery, and had risen to a managerial position at the factory that employed him. He came home with a trade, professional contacts and some twelve thousand lire in savings.

The first workshop employed five or six people. By his death forty years later, the industrial activities he had founded employed roughly two thousand across ten plants, with annual turnover of thirty billion lire, according to the historian Ercole Sori. A business that began with scales had moved into LPG cylinders, cookers, water heaters and other domestic appliances.

The Fabriano area had changed with it. Factories had reached villages and rural settlements, bringing paid work closer to home and helping to build manufacturing skills that would shape the region for generations.

The conditions of this transformation matter. Sori documents low wages and the absence of insurance and trade-union protection among the first Albacina workers. Later, by locating plants near people's homes, the firm allowed many employees to maintain their ties to family and land. Sori also suggests that the rural economy may have helped the business keep labour costs down.

Production was dispersed across the territory; managerial authority remained concentrated. A company could be deeply rooted in a place without giving its workers a corresponding voice in its decisions.

Merloni was also chairman of the local savings bank, mayor of Fabriano and a senator of the Republic, holding these offices at different points in his life. In 1963 he set up a Foundation to promote new productive activity in inland Marche.

After Merloni's death, his industrial legacy took several paths. The company developed by Antonio eventually entered a deep crisis. Vittorio's business grew into the international Indesit Company before passing to Whirlpool, while the heating and water-heating operations developed by Francesco continued to expand into today's Ariston Group. The Foundation became an important centre for research into the Marche economy.

These outcomes came decades apart, under different leaders and market conditions. None can stand for the whole of the founder's experience.

**A firm can create work without asking a population to leave its home territory. What remains to be understood is how much of that development stays with the community when the economic conditions that made it possible change.**

His life brings the question into focus through factories, working lives and public institutions. To answer it, we have to examine the productive capacity he built, the conditions under which it grew, and what of it endured after the businesses changed.

## 1. Aristide Merloni at a glance

**Aristide Merloni (1897–1970)** was an Italian entrepreneur and politician who helped bring about the manufacturing transformation of inland Marche in the twentieth century.

He was born on 24 October 1897 in Albacina, in the municipality of Fabriano. After qualifying as an industrial technician (*perito industriale*) at the Montani Institute in Fermo, he worked, after the First World War, in Piedmont at the Buroni factory in Pinerolo, which specialised in weighing instruments.

In 1930 he returned to Albacina and started a small scales business. In 1933 he founded Società anonima Merloni Aristide (SAMA), at first organised on a family basis. The firm consolidated in Fabriano and, after the Second World War, began to diversify.

In 1953 he met Enrico Mattei. The following year he built a plant in Matelica to make LPG cylinders, and further industrial activities grew out of that work.

Merloni chaired the Cassa di Risparmio di Fabriano (the local savings bank) from 1943 to 1951, was mayor of the town from 1951 to 1957, and sat in the Senate for Christian Democracy (DC) from 1958 until his death. In 1963 he established the Aristide Merloni Foundation to support economic development and employment in the inland areas of the Marche.

He died on 19 December 1970 in a road accident.

Historians have studied his enterprise as an example of *industrializzazione diffusa*—diffuse industrialisation: relatively small, specialised plants close to workers' homes, coordinated by one management.

It brought manufacturing jobs into a rural area, alongside marked inequalities of power. The earliest wages, the concentration of decisions and the relationship between business and public institutions all belong to its history.

## 2. Why he matters

Merloni helped establish a substantial manufacturing base far from Italy's main industrial centres. Fabriano already had a strong tradition of papermaking and mechanical engineering; his businesses extended that industrial inheritance into new products and brought factory work to a wider range of settlements.

The skills first concentrated in making weighing instruments were gradually used in other sectors. Metalworking, sheet-metal forming and fabrication led to cylinders and then to appliances for the home. Industrialisation advanced through the transfer of know-how between products, the opening of new markets and increasing specialisation of the plants.

The organisation of work was shaped by the place itself. Families lived across Fabriano and its surrounding villages, and poor roads made the daily journey to a single large factory difficult. Merloni established specialised plants at several locations. Workers could remain near their homes; the company, for its part, could draw on the resources of a rural economy. The benefits and the costs of proximity were not necessarily shared equally.

His story also includes a significant public role. Through the savings bank, the town council and the Senate he held institutional responsibilities that, over time, intertwined with the growth of his business.

The 1963 Foundation added an attempt to widen development beyond the original businesses by supporting the birth of other firms. The results of its first interventions were uneven and call for separate assessment.

The lasting question is therefore not simply how the firm grew. It is whether the work, skills and opportunities generated by that growth became resources the territory could retain when the firm itself changed.

## 3. The human question — Being able to stay

For generations, people from Italy's inland areas often had to leave to learn a trade or find better-paid work. Industrial cities offered prospects that farming and small local businesses could seldom match. Merloni followed that route to Pinerolo, where he gained skills and a professional standing he was unlikely to have achieved in Albacina. His return in 1930 made it possible to put those skills to work at home, and his small venture gradually began employing others.

They do, however, show the emergence of a sizeable source of work where industrial opportunities had previously been more limited.

To stay can mean keeping a home, family ties and a familiar way of life; that is no small gain when departure is the only other choice. Yet work close to home is only as good as the wages, protections and room for advancement it offers. A major employer can anchor a community and leave it exposed when too many livelihoods depend on a handful of decisions. The ability to stay ultimately involves a further freedom: to adapt when the economy changes without surrendering the skills a place has built up.

## 4. A life in outline

### 1897–1919. Formation and war

Aristide Merloni was born in Albacina on 24 October 1897, the son of Antonio Merloni and Ester Stopponi.

He grew up in a modest rural middle-class household: his father sympathised with socialism, while his mother came from a deeply religious background. As a teenager he became active in local Catholic associations. In 1911 he founded in Albacina the circle *Religione e patria* ("Religion and Fatherland"), linked to the Catholic associational milieu of Fabriano.

He attended the industrial school in Fabriano and then the Montani Institute in Fermo, where in 1916 he earned his diploma as an industrial technician.

He served in the First World War in the Engineers corps and returned to Albacina in 1919. In 1921 he married Maria Mattioli.

### The 1920s. Piedmont and industrial apprenticeship

In the years after the war he moved to Piedmont, where he worked at Buroni in Pinerolo, a maker of industrial scales, platform scales and steelyards.

At Buroni he learnt the techniques of production, the management of a factory and the commercial side of industrial work. He supplemented his wages with work for smaller workshops connected to the company.

In 1925 he became manager of the plant.

In 1924 he had drawn up, on Buroni's behalf, a factory project for weighing instruments and mechanical construction. Sori sees in it a first reflection on the possibility of building a competitive small firm.

### 1930–1939. The return and the first firm

In 1930 he went back to Albacina with his wife and three children: Ester, born in 1922, Francesco in 1925 and Antonio in 1926. Vittorio would be born in 1933.

He had about twelve thousand lire in savings, backed by promissory notes signed by the village priest.

He started a small production of weighing instruments with five or six employees.

According to Sori, pay was very modest and workers had no insurance or union protection.

SAMA was founded in 1933 on a family basis. Merloni's father handled payroll, stores and purchasing; his brother-in-law acted as foreman.

In 1936 the business moved to Fabriano, where employees rose to about forty and, according to Sori, were paid better than in Albacina.

Annual turnover, about eighty thousand lire in 1931, reached half a million by 1938.

The products began to establish themselves on the national market, though without yet reaching the scale they would have after the war.

### 1940–1949. War and reconstruction

The Second World War interrupted expansion. The firm struggled with raw-material supplies and labour shortages.

In 1943–1944 the plant was damaged by bombing, a fire and removals by German troops. Production stopped and, after the Liberation, the premises were requisitioned by the Allied command.

On 10 July 1943 Merloni had been named chairman of the Cassa di Risparmio di Fabriano. During reconstruction the bank helped finance economic recovery and public housing.

In 1949 an order from the State Railways for sixty weighbridges helped relaunch industrial activity.

Between 1947 and 1949 Merloni also bought six farms, ending up with about 120 hectares of land.

### 1951–1958. Business and administration

In 1951 Merloni was elected mayor of Fabriano for Christian Democracy. He left the chairmanship of the savings bank because the two offices were incompatible.

At this stage the firm employed about sixty workers and had reached a very high share of the national market for industrial platform scales.

In 1953 he met Enrico Mattei in Fabriano.

The following year a plant was built in Matelica for LPG cylinders.

Re-elected mayor in 1956 and elected provincial councillor, Merloni left the mayoralty in 1957 because of incompatibility.

In 1958 he was elected to the Senate of the Republic.

### 1958–1970. Diversification, politics and the Foundation

In the years of the Italian economic miracle the industrial activities expanded towards new products.

Growth gradually involved his sons, who took on different production and management responsibilities.

In 1963 Merloni established the Aristide Merloni Foundation to support new entrepreneurial activity in the inland areas.

He served three consecutive terms as senator, sitting on parliamentary committees that also covered industry and trade.

In his last years he backed expansion and diversification projects that would keep the group busy after his death.

He died on 19 December 1970 in a road accident.

According to Sori's account in the *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani* (Biographical Dictionary of Italians), the industrial activities then employed about two thousand people, with ten plants and thirty billion lire in turnover.

## 5. What his life reveals about the enterprise

Merloni's formation helps explain how an individual professional experience could become entrepreneurial capacity.

At Pinerolo he saw an organisation in which the main plant worked alongside small specialised workshops. What he learned concerned the building of the instruments as much as sourcing and marketing.

The return to Albacina came from a combination of skills, economic opportunity and personal relationships. Sori recognises in his civic formation a sense of responsibility towards the local community, but regards the aims of entrepreneurial emancipation already present in the 1924 project as only probable.

The sources do not allow us to say with certainty how much economic and how much social motives weighed in the 1930 decision.

What we can observe is that Merloni started a business with limited resources, using the knowledge he had accumulated and the professional relationships he had kept in Piedmont.

Even after his return, the business continued to buy parts and finished instruments from Buroni and resell them under the Merloni name. Its local foundations were built through a commercial network that still reached back to Piedmont.

A second biographical element is land ownership. Between 1947 and 1949 Merloni bought six farms, 120 hectares in all.

This shows a direct economic tie to the agrarian structure of the area. It does not prove, by itself, a paternalistic purpose or a particular wage policy, but it makes the relationship between industrial property and rural society more complex.

Finally, public activity ran alongside business activity through successive offices at the savings bank, in local government and in Parliament.

The length of this commitment documents a significant institutional presence. Its meaning has to be judged through the decisions taken and the effects observable, distinguishing public interest from the firm's own aims.

## 6. Inland Marche and industrial change

Fabriano had a manufacturing identity before Industrie Merloni existed.

The paper-making tradition and mechanical engineering had created productive skills, a culture of industrial work and forms of economic organisation relatively advanced compared with many other inland areas of the Marche.

Beyond the town centre, Fabriano's population was scattered among hamlets and small rural settlements, many poorly connected to the main industrial sites.

In the post-war decades industrial transformation took place within this geography. The growth of manufacturing gradually changed the relations between farm work, wage employment and population mobility.

Merloni's factories were one part of this transformation. By specialising plants and placing them in different settlements under a single management, the business could employ people from smaller communities without requiring them to move permanently to a city.

The coexistence of industrial work and the rural family economy marked a part of the transformation. Many workers kept ties to farming, to home ownership and to family activities.

Historians and economists have examined this pattern as diffuse industrialisation and, later, *industrializzazione senza fratture* ("industrialisation without fractures"). The latter term, especially associated with Giorgio Fuà and Carlo Zacchia, describes industrial growth that does not immediately sweep away older social structures.

Territorial continuity should not be confused with the absence of conflict.

Between 1955 and 1965, Sori recalls, Fabriano went through a phase of strong industrial tension, linked among other things to the difficulties of the Miliani paper mills and the crisis of important engineering firms such as Maglio-Fiorentini.

The expansion of the Merloni business thus took place alongside other, less favourable economic stories.

The so-called “Marche model” grew through the interaction of businesses, labour, changing families, markets and institutions. Merloni was an important part of its history, neither its sole origin nor its complete explanation.

## 7. Circles of inquiry

### Economic history — The accumulation of capabilities

The growth of mechanical production brings skills, capital, markets and diversification into the same field of view. The Merloni case shows how familiar industrial processes can find new applications, and how productive organisation feeds competitiveness.

### Economic geography — The territory inside the firm

The distribution of plants reveals the weight of population, transport and local conditions in industrial choices. A factory is shaped by its setting even as it changes the economy around it.

### Sociology of work — The worker-peasant

The link between manufacturing employment and the persistence of the rural family economy lets us study wages, daily time, occupational mobility and dependence on industrial work.

### Political science — Representation and development

Merloni's public offices offer a case for analysing the relationship between private initiative, political consensus and the administration of a territory.

### Business history — Ownership and succession

The growth of Industrie Merloni shows how a family structure handles specialisation, diversification and the distribution of managerial responsibility. The history of the successors also lets us separate continuity of ownership from continuity of productive activity.

### Development economics — The durability of value

The presence of a firm can change incomes and employment. Whether skills, institutions and new entrepreneurial capacity endure is a further question, to be tested with data and long-run comparison.

## 8. Documents of an entrepreneurial life

Aristide Merloni left no body of systematic theoretical work comparable to that of a political thinker. His story is reconstructed mainly from industrial choices, company documents, institutional records and historical scholarship.

### The 1924 industrial project

In 1924 Merloni drew up for Buroni of Pinerolo a *Progetto di fabbrica strumenti per pesare e costruzioni meccaniche* (project for a factory making weighing instruments and mechanical constructions). Ercole Sori reads it as a first formulation of an entrepreneurial strategy built on the characteristics of a specific market.

The project explored how a relatively small company could compete in a market where the capital required was modest, returns could be favourable, specialist knowledge mattered and government authorisation was necessary for work with weights and measures. Those regulatory conditions were a barrier to entry that could protect operators who were already skilled and licensed.

He wrote the proposal for Buroni. Sori considers it possible that the project also expressed Merloni's ambition to establish himself independently.

**The original 1924 text has not been consulted directly.** Our knowledge of the document and of its purposes comes from Sori's published reconstruction.

### Industrial documentation

Corporate deeds, commercial contracts, accounting records and correspondence would be decisive sources for reconstructing production choices. Some photographic documentation of the plants can be identified in Italy's national archival systems. The actual availability, extent and accessibility of individual company files still need archival verification.

### Public acts

His offices at the savings bank, in Fabriano's town hall and in the Senate form a second documentary core. Official parliamentary records confirm the three terms and membership of the committees covering industry, trade and tourism. Local administrative records could allow deeper study of his time as mayor and of the relationship between economic development and political representation.

### The 1963 Foundation

The creation of the Aristide Merloni Foundation is evidence of an intention to promote new economic initiatives in inland Marche. Studying its first interventions would require original documents on financing, equity stakes, firms supported and employment results.

### Later reconstructions

Corporate and family memoirs are useful for identifying events, people and decisions. They need to be checked against independent studies, especially when they attribute personal motives or judgements of success.

## 9. The return of skills

*An original Cerchi d’inchiostro reading*

Merloni's industrial story begins with a skill acquired far from the territory where it would later be used.

At Pinerolo he learned to build weighing instruments, saw how a factory was organised, and worked with small workshops in its supply chain. When he returned to Albacina he had technical and commercial knowledge that could be turned into an independent business. His industrial experience, savings and local contacts gave him the means to begin.

The continuing link with Buroni deserves attention. For some years Merloni bought components and finished products from his former employer and resold them under his own brand. Skills were therefore transferred through a network of productive, commercial and personal ties connecting Piedmont and the Marche.

Later growth required an organisation able to carry out ever more complex work. What one person knew how to do became a business through the work of others: workers, suppliers and customers, supported by machinery and increasingly specialised production. New jobs grew out of that organisation.

In the present state of research there is no documentary series that would let us measure how many technicians, entrepreneurs or new firms came directly from experience gained at Industrie Merloni. We can document industrial and employment growth; the spread of skills beyond the group remains a question to explore.

The distinction matters. Knowledge can be used by many people without all of them sharing in the ownership or decisions of the firm. It can also become a wider territorial resource when it feeds suppliers, new businesses and training institutions.

The evidence carries us as far as the transformation of one man's skills into an organised industrial capacity. Whether those skills became an independent asset of the territory, able to flourish beyond the group, is a further proposition that the available sources cannot yet establish.

The question that remains is whether productive knowledge can become an asset able to outlive the organisations that carried it.

## 10. The nearby factory and the cost of proximity

*An original Cerchi d’inchiostro reading*

In the post-war Fabriano area, the spread of the population across town, villages and hamlets was a concrete constraint on industry. Roads were poor, and daily commuting could affect the availability of workers.

Merloni answered part of that problem by placing specialised plants in several settlements while keeping strategic direction in one management.

In 1954 the cylinder plant was built at Matelica, in open countryside. Between 1957 and 1959 activities developed at Cerreto d'Esi; in 1965 the Pianello di Genga plant, for water heaters, came into operation. Santa Maria di Fabriano and Melano-Marischio followed, between the late 1960s and 1970.

Each location met different production needs, but each was chosen in relation to the work carried out there and the homes of prospective employees.

### The worker-peasant

Proximity to the plants allowed many workers to go on living in their own homes and to keep ties to the family farm economy. Manufacturing employment fitted into a structure in which house, land and domestic activities all contributed to household subsistence.

Ercole Sori also reads this arrangement in relation to cost containment. In his view, the difficulty of commuting and the economies available in the rural setting could have made it convenient for the firm to place plants near the workers, avoiding wage pressures linked to mobility and to the higher cost of urban living.

This is a historiographical interpretation explicitly attributed to Sori, not a documented statement by Aristide Merloni about his own intentions.

For the first employees in 1930 Sori documents low pay and no protections. For 1936 he notes instead that workers moved to the Fabriano plant were paid better than in Albacina. The documentation consulted contains no comparative wage series or contracts that would let us quantify the effect of location on labour cost in later decades.

The question of labour costs matters, but it cannot be treated as if it had been measured in the same way as the locations of plants and their employment figures.

### Distributed production, unified direction

The industrial model combined relatively small plants, production specialisation and corporate coordination. It delivered economies of production without concentrating the whole organisation in one site.

Sori sees in it a search for industrial advantage at territorial scale, through the division of tasks and the keeping of a single direction. The distribution of workplaces did not imply a matching distribution of ownership or of strategic decisions.

### Beyond local economies

In his last years Merloni also took part in negotiations for new plants due to open in 1971, after his death. Sori reports projects at Melano-Marischio and in the Rieti area, tied to the opportunities offered by the Cassa per il Mezzogiorno (the national fund for southern Italy's development) and by new forms of financing.

This shows that industrial location could also depend on public incentives and conditions external to the local economic system. The model adapted to places but did not stay still: expansion progressively required different resources and counterparts.

### Historical meaning

The organisation of the plants created employment opportunities in inland Marche. Its effects on whether families stayed, on wages and on the distribution of opportunity have to be assessed separately.

A factory close to home could reshape daily life. Whether its gains were widely shared depended on wages, working conditions and the scope for further economic activity. The proximity that made work possible for rural families might also have made production cheaper for the company; that tension is central to the history of Merloni's model.

## 11. Mattei, Matelica and diversification

In 1953 Aristide Merloni met Enrico Mattei in Fabriano.

Mattei, born at Acqualagna in 1906 and raised in Matelica, was then chairman of ENI, Italy's state energy group. According to Sori, he wanted to create jobs directly in the economically depressed mountain areas of the provinces of Ancona and Macerata.

Merloni had already built a well-established business in weighing instruments. The meeting was thus between a leading figure of national public industry and a private entrepreneur with productive capacity of his own.

The prospect of new work was tied to the expansion of the gas and hydrocarbons market. According to Sori, Mattei first proposed building tanks for fuel stations, backed by a promised AGIP order (AGIP being ENI's fuel and gas arm). Merloni preferred to turn to LPG cylinders for household use, a product that could make use of mechanical skills he already had.

In 1954 a plant for the new product was built at Matelica.

### The controversy over the AGIP orders

The published accounts disagree over the AGIP business.

Sori reports an order for seventy thousand cylinders held out by AGIP gas that was never realised, because ENI was at the same time caught up in the affairs of Nuovo Pignone in Florence. A retrospective account by Mario Bartocci, published in 2013 in a volume by the Rotary Club of Fabriano, instead mentions two AGIP orders, of fifty thousand and a hundred thousand pieces.

These are not equivalent kinds of evidence. Sori offers a specialist historical reconstruction; Bartocci writes from later local memory. Sori's account carries greater historiographical weight, although that does not resolve what was ordered, delivered or paid for.

**It is not possible to establish whether the references concern the same orders, different moments, or incompatible accounts of a single relationship.**

The original documentation on contracts, deliveries and payments has not been consulted. This guide therefore does not take as definitively established either the fulfilment or the non-fulfilment of all the AGIP supplies.

### The success of the cylinders

Whatever the controversy, the Matelica plant developed a production that reached considerable size.

According to Sori, at full capacity it turned out ten thousand cylinders a day, quickly winning about a quarter of the national market. By 1970 the share is said to have risen to sixty per cent.

This scale built up skills and financial resources that could be used for new activities. Cylinder production required metalworking that was transferable to other products: from fabrication and sheet forming the firm moved on to water heaters and other applications, while enamelling techniques found use in cooking appliances and metal household furnishings.

Diversification also followed commercial openings in the home market. In the 1950s and 1960s the firm entered stoves, cookers, water heaters, kitchen furniture, refrigerators, washing machines and dishwashers, through different mixes of in-house production, acquisitions, licences and outside supply. The process gradually involved Francesco, Antonio and Vittorio Merloni.

### The meeting's place in the triad

Mattei pursued an industrial policy that used the economic capacity of a large public body. Merloni answered the opportunities through a private entrepreneurial organisation built in the preceding decades.

The meeting in Fabriano illustrates what an opening created by public enterprise could offer a private manufacturer—and how much still depended on decisions the manufacturer made independently. The later success of the cylinders also came from the ability to reach markets independent of the original AGIP prospect.

## 12. Business, credit and local power

Merloni's public presence developed through different offices, held in succession while his business went on.

On 10 July 1943 he became chairman of the Cassa di Risparmio di Fabriano. The war had caused heavy damage to the town and its productive activities. According to Sori, during reconstruction the bank helped finance economic recovery and public housing, in a town that had lost thousands of dwelling rooms.

Merloni left the chairmanship in 1951, when he was elected mayor for Christian Democracy.

### Votes in the hamlets

His political rise took place in a territory with working-class traditions and a strong left, but also a large rural population. Sori gives, for 1951, about 13,940 residents in the rural areas against 14,077 in the urban centre, so the hamlets were almost as populous as the town.

At the 1951 local elections Merloni won, according to Sori, 8,448 votes out of 17,627 voters. The figure has to be read in the political context of the post-war years, when changes in national balances also shaped local administrations.

In 1951 the firm employed about sixty workers, and its later spread of plants had not yet reached the scale of the 1960s. It would therefore be improper to attribute the electoral result to an industrial employment already spread across the countryside. Sori links Merloni's success instead to the readiness of the rural hamlets for new forms of development and to the prospect of production sites nearer where people lived. The prospect of industrial change, in other words, helped shape political expectations before the new factories had arrived.

### Administration

According to Sori, during his term as mayor the council adopted tax measures favourable to poorer taxpayers, including 2,400 tax exemptions and the removal of some surcharges. The municipal budget reached balance, helped by extraordinary state funding.

These are part of the documented administrative record. A full assessment would require comparison with council records and with the political and economic context of the time.

Re-elected in 1956 and elected provincial councillor, Merloni left the mayoralty in 1957 for incompatibility. In 1958 he entered the Senate for Christian Democracy and stayed until his death. Official parliamentary records confirm his membership of the 9th permanent committee, responsible for industry, trade and tourism, in the 3rd and 4th legislatures, and of the 10th permanent committee in the 5th.

### The relationship between firm and institutions

Merloni's story shows entrepreneurial activity continuing through three successive spheres of public responsibility: credit, local government and Parliament. This continuity made him a major figure in the economic and political life of the Fabriano area.

The relationship between the firm's interests and the general interest must nonetheless be studied through concrete decisions, avoiding both the presumption of a natural coincidence and the attribution of undocumented conflicts of interest.

Industrial growth, the consensus of the hamlets and presence in institutions belong to the same historical context. Their relations have to be reconstructed, not deduced automatically from the biography.

## 13. The Foundation — From enterprise to economic knowledge

In 1963 Aristide Merloni established the Foundation that bears his name.

According to Sori, the initial endowment was twenty million lire and the field of action covered twenty-six municipalities in inland Marche. Management was entrusted to his son Francesco.

The original aim was to promote new entrepreneurial activity and support employment in the region's inland territories. The project addressed a concrete problem: the growth of a single firm could contribute to local economic development, but the presence of other activities would widen productive opportunities.

### The original initiatives

The Foundation intervened to favour new firms, including through risk-capital participation. The effectiveness of these interventions needs a separate assessment from the intentions. Sori observes that several initiatives had unsatisfactory or failed results. Reconstructing individual projects, the capital deployed and the activities that actually survived would require access to the original documentation.

Establishing the Foundation showed an intention to encourage growth beyond the family business. Its results have to be judged separately from that intention.

### After the founder

Aristide's death in 1970 did not interrupt the institution's work. Under Francesco Merloni's direction the Foundation gradually widened its interests towards economic research and the study of development models.

In the 1970s it contributed to the debate on the *Via Adriatica allo Sviluppo* (the "Adriatic Road to Development"), with the involvement of scholars of the regional economy. In 1975 *Economia Marche* was born, at first a series of monographic notebooks on the regional economy; it later grew into a periodic scientific journal, today's *Economia Marche – Journal of Applied Economics*. These initiatives belong to the Foundation's later history and should not be attributed to Aristide personally.

### The ranking of the main Marche firms

Since 1986 the Foundation has carried out a systematic collection, analysis and interpretation of the financial statements of the main firms of the Marche. Over time it has built an economic and financial archive covering more than five hundred companies.

The annual ranking considers the region's largest companies by size and territorial criteria defined in the research methodology, accompanied by economic and financial analyses produced with the Department of Engineering of the Marche Polytechnic University. On 29 October 2026 the fortieth edition was presented.

This sustained record gives researchers a way to follow the changing economy of the Marche. Being able to compare balance sheets, sectors, company sizes and results lets us study transformations that go beyond the history of a single industrial family.

The different perimeters used must however be kept distinct: consolidated accounts of large international groups may include activities carried out outside the Marche, while ordinary accounts often represent a narrower company perimeter. A position in the ranking is therefore not a direct measure of wealth, employment or investment concentrated in the region.

### Institutional continuity and independence of research

After Francesco Merloni's death in 2024, the Foundation's presidency passed to Paolo Merloni, executive chairman of Ariston Group. Holding both roles makes it necessary to distinguish the Foundation's memory and institutional direction from the scientific assessment of economic results.

Research produced with universities and scholars can be judged through its methods, data and the possibility of independent comparison. The family link should be declared, without automatically turning it into a negative judgement on the quality of the scientific work.

The Foundation represents a legacy different from the plants: it produces knowledge, organises archives and contributes to the debate on regional economic change. Its history shows an institutional continuity across several generations, changing purposes and instruments relative to the original project.

## 14. Key ideas

**Work changes the geography of possibility.** Manufacturing close to where people live can widen professional opportunity in inland areas.

**Industrial skills travel between territories.** Merloni's Piedmont experience was one of the conditions that made it possible to start the business in the Marche.

**Growth needs organisation as well as knowledge.** Capital, suppliers, markets and workers turn individual skills into collective productive capacity.

**The distribution of plants answers to economic and geographic conditions.** Plants close to the rural population offered work and possible organisational advantages to the firm.

**Industrialisation changes the society it enters.** The worker-peasant represents a transition in which industrial work and the resources of the rural economy continue to coexist.

**Diversification uses transferable knowledge.** Skills in mechanics and metalworking favoured entry into new sectors.

**Entrepreneurial activity can go hand in hand with public responsibility.** The Merloni case lets us study the continuity between industrial initiative and presence in institutions.

**A common industrial origin can lead to different developments.** The sons' firms followed distinct paths, shaped by later decisions, markets and historical conditions.

**Territorial development needs a test of durability.** The growth of a firm and the persistence of a community's economic capabilities are connected questions, but they are measured by different kinds of evidence.

## 15. Key concepts

**Productive rootedness.** The process by which an industrial activity settles in a territory, using and changing its skills, employment, infrastructure and economic relationships.

**Diffuse industrialisation.** The growth of manufacturing through production units spread among urban centres and smaller settlements.

**Worker-peasant.** A worker who combines industrial employment with the persistence of activities or resources tied to the rural family.

**Diversification by technological affinity.** Widening production through knowledge and processes that carry over between different products and markets.

**Family firm.** An organisation in which ownership, management and succession are closely tied to a family.

**Territorial economies.** Productive advantages arising from the location and the economic and social features of an area.

**Territorial development.** The economic and social transformation of an area, observable through work, incomes, skills, infrastructure, firms and institutions.

**Productive dependence.** A condition in which a large share of a territory's economic opportunities is tied to a few firms or specialisations.

**Industrial continuity.** The persistence and transformation of productive capacity through innovation, succession, corporate reorganisation and market change.

These categories belong mainly to historiography, to economics and to the editorial reading of Cerchi d'inchiostro. They are not a theoretical system formulated by Aristide Merloni himself.

## 16. The Constellation

### Origins

**Technical industrial culture.** The Montani Institute in Fermo and the learning of mechanical work formed the base of the first business.

**Social Catholicism.** Youth associations and the influence of Popolarismo (the Catholic-inspired political movement of the early twentieth century) helped shape Merloni's civic and political formation.

**Industrial Piedmont.** His time at Buroni in Pinerolo taught him processes, organisations and specialised markets.

**Rural Marche society.** The pattern of settlement and the family economies helped define what forms of productive organisation were possible.

### Contemporaries

**Enrico Mattei.** A direct interlocutor in the diversification of the 1950s. The relationship shows the meeting of public economic capacity and private industrial initiative. (The previous node of the twelfth triad.)

**Adriano Olivetti.** A contemporary who developed an explicit reflection on community and on the political responsibilities of enterprise. The comparison with Merloni concerns two different ways of intervening in social transformation. Olivetti formulated a political and cultural project set out in writings and public initiatives; Merloni developed an entrepreneurial and institutional practice that has to be reconstructed mainly from his decisions. The difference describes the characteristics of the available sources and of the two bodies of work, and sets up no hierarchy between them.

**Local administrations and Christian Democracy.** The political context in which Merloni built part of his institutional presence.

**The labour movement and trade unions.** Actors necessary for a complete reconstruction of the labour relations and conflicts of Fabriano's industrial society.

### The legacy

**Francesco Merloni.** Development of the heating and water-heating sector, the growth of Ariston and the widening of the Foundation's activities.

**Antonio Merloni.** Development of his own industrial business and the later crisis of the group.

**Vittorio Merloni.** International expansion in household appliances and the history of Merloni Elettrodomestici, later Indesit Company.

**Paolo Merloni.** Continuity of the heating branch through Ariston Group, and current chairmanship of the Foundation.

**Giorgio Fuà and the scholars of Marche industrialisation.** The development of the categories used to interpret territorial economies and diffused manufacturing systems.

**The Aristide Merloni Foundation.** An institution that over time has developed research, documentation, training and observation of the regional production system.

### Contemporary phenomena

The transformation of global value chains, the mobility of capital, industrial skills, technological research, the depopulation of inland areas and the vulnerability of specialised territories. How these issues relate to the Merloni story belongs to the interpretation of the present. It attributes to the founder no judgements on phenomena after his death.

## 17. Three industrial legacies

When Aristide Merloni died in 1970 the family firm had acquired an articulated productive structure. His sons had long taken part in management and had developed different skills and responsibilities.

What followed was not predetermined. It depended on entrepreneurial decisions, markets, ownership and competitive conditions belonging to later generations.

### Antonio Merloni — Autonomy and crisis

Antonio Merloni, born in 1926, worked in the family firm on the mechanical side. In 1968 he started an industrial initiative of his own, aimed at making washing machines. The business later grew in household appliances, including production for other brands.

Antonio Merloni SpA reached considerable size and contributed to manufacturing employment in territories of both the Marche and Umbria. On 14 October 2008 the company was admitted to extraordinary administration (Italy's special insolvency procedure for large companies). The crisis involved thousands of workers and a wide system of supplier firms. In the following years procedures were opened to sell company units and attempts at reindustrialisation were made, with complex and controversial results.

The case belongs to the group's own history and has to be studied through its financial structure, the competitive conditions of the sector and the decisions taken by successive managements.

### Vittorio Merloni — Internationalisation and changes of ownership

Vittorio Merloni, born in 1933, joined the family business in 1960 and contributed to the commercial development of the household-appliance sector. After his father's death he took responsibility for the division.

Merloni Elettrodomestici became one of Europe's leading groups in the sector and later took the name Indesit Company. International growth gradually changed the size and geography of production.

In 2014 Whirlpool acquired control of Indesit, initially taking 60.4% of the capital, corresponding to 66.8% of the voting rights. The operation placed the business under international industrial ownership.

The ownership changes continued. In 2024, under an agreement between Whirlpool and Arçelik, Beko Europe was created, initially controlled 75% by the Turkish group and 25% held by Whirlpool. On 23 June 2026 Arçelik announced that, through its subsidiary Beko B.V., it had completed the acquisition of the remaining stake for 71.45 million euros, reaching 100% control of the company. The new company absorbed European activities that had belonged to Whirlpool, including Italian production and plants from Indesit's industrial chain.

This series of acquisitions helps explain why industrial disputes in the Fabriano area now involve different companies and brands, although parts of their history still lead back to Vittorio's business. Responsibility for individual decisions must stay with the respective industrial actors.

### Francesco Merloni — The continuity of heating and water heating

Francesco Merloni, born in 1925, was Aristide's first son. A graduate in engineering, he joined the family business in the 1950s and took charge of the Matelica plant. In 1970 he became head of the sanitaryware division and later contributed to the development of the heating and water-heating businesses.

From this branch came Merloni Termosanitari, Ariston Thermo and today's Ariston Group. Francesco Merloni died in 2024. His son Paolo is now the group's executive chairman.

Ariston is an international company in thermal-comfort technologies, water heating and energy solutions. In 2025 it recorded consolidated revenue of 2,707.1 million euros, up 2.8% on 2024, with organic growth of 3%. In the fortieth edition of the ranking of the main Marche companies, presented in October 2026 by the Aristide Merloni Foundation, Ariston ranks first in the region by turnover. In July 2026 it also completed the acquisition of Riello Group.

The figures show the scale and continued industrial activity of one branch of the Merloni family businesses. Reading them territorially, however, needs a fundamental distinction: consolidated revenue includes activities in many countries and does not directly measure the economic value generated in the Marche.

### The industrial presence in the Fabriano area

Ariston keeps productive and technological activities in the area. In 2025 the group announced reinforcement of research and development at Albacina and Fabriano, production at Cerreto d'Esi and Arcevia, and investment in new industrial processes.

The new Albacina plant, for electric water heaters, was presented as part of a multi-year investment plan in Italy worth five hundred million euros for 2022–2028. It is meant to replace the Genga plant in its main productive function, for which conversion activities have been envisaged. A new building, however, does not necessarily mean additional production or jobs for the area. To assess the effects one needs data on actual output, the workers involved, the functions transferred and any additional activities.

The group also declares moves to bring certain processes back to Italy that were previously carried out abroad, including production linked to heat pumps and water heaters. This information documents orientations and activities communicated by the company; judging their economic and social value requires independent verification.

### One origin, different outcomes

The three trajectories show how far an industrial inheritance can change over time. One branch went through a deep crisis; another passed under the control of international groups; the third kept a family continuity while developing new productive and commercial capacity.

These outcomes are not a moral ranking of the entrepreneurs. They belong to different corporate histories, shaped by markets, products, investments and decisions that followed one another for decades after Aristide's death. Taken together, they let us separate the continuity of the family origin from the continuity of firms, ownership and local productive capacity.

## 18. The contemporary wound — What stays with the territory

Fabriano's industrial history is going through a deep transformation.

For decades manufacturing helped create work, skills and productive relationships in an inland Apennine territory. Later crises changed the distribution of activities and the relationship between firms and communities. The story involves different companies, each with its own industrial history, but their territorial consequences meet in the same geographic area.

### A measurable transformation

The report *Destinazione Fabriano*, prepared by Nomisma with the municipality and published in 2025, documents the change in the local economy. Between 2016 and 2023 the number of people employed in manufacturing local units in the municipality of Fabriano fell by 2,682, or 43%. Over the same period employment in all local units in the municipality fell by 8%.

The gap between the two indicators shows a differentiated transformation: manufacturing losses were far sharper than the fall in overall local employment. Nomisma places these dynamics within the framework of productive specialisation, globalisation, industrial crises and the economic and territorial effects of the 2016–2017 earthquake and of the pandemic. In its analysis of the period, strong dependence on specific industrial sectors emerges as a factor of vulnerability compared with more diversified territories.

These are figures for the municipality over a defined period. They cannot be read as a balance sheet of employment in the Merloni companies.

### Firms and population

Later indicators show further difficulties, though they describe different phenomena. According to CNA Marche's research centre, in 2023 Fabriano recorded 184 fewer firms than the year before, down 8%. In 2024 the contraction was 46 firms, or 2.2%, so the fall continued but with less intensity. In 2025 the balance of the Fabriano area stayed negative, while the municipality of Fabriano recorded a 1.2% fall in the number of firms against 2024.

The town's population has also moved downward over the long run. Nomisma documents an 8.2% fall in residents over the decade 2013–2023, with a significant role for net migration and population ageing.

Data on firm numbers, employment and population measure different dimensions. Read together, they let us recognise a persistent territorial difficulty without building causal relationships the sources do not directly demonstrate.

### The industrial transformations of 2026

In autumn 2026 several industrial disputes were unfolding in the Fabriano area.

At Melano, Beko Europe remained the subject of talks among the company, workers and institutions, including over contested delays in carrying out the 2025 industrial plan. The company arises from the 2024 agreement between Whirlpool and Arçelik and is linked, through earlier changes of ownership, to part of the activities historically developed by Indesit.

At Cerreto d'Esi, Electrolux initially proposed closing its plant. In October 2026 the talks went on to introduce hypotheses for keeping the site through a reindustrialisation path. No definitive agreement had yet been reached.

At Fabriano, Whirlpool Technology announced its decision to end the activities of its research and design centre, where about forty specialised technicians and engineers work. Institutions have opened talks on ways of safeguarding employment.

The three cases concern different functions of manufacturing: production, the continuity of plants and design capacity. The last is especially important. A technical centre holds knowledge that takes time, training and experience to accumulate, and closing it can affect the future availability of skills even when other production continues.

### The capabilities that keep developing

The condition of the territory also includes industrial and technological activities that remain significant. Ariston Group keeps a productive presence in the Marche and continues to invest in new technologies and industrial processes. The activities announced in 2025 include research at Albacina and Fabriano, heat-pump production, water heaters and the manufacture of electronic components.

The continuity of these activities is an important part of Fabriano's present industrial story. To assess their actual contribution to the territory, though, one must distinguish announced investments from completed interventions, transfers of existing production from additional capacity. A group's worldwide revenue tells us little, by itself, about the workers or production still based in its place of origin.

### The question of durability

Aristide Merloni's story lets us observe how a technical skill acquired elsewhere became local industrial capacity. Contemporary transformations raise a further question: what happens when some activities move, change ownership or cease operating?

Jobs and incomes may be the most visible losses. Harder to measure is what happens to the training of new technicians, the survival of suppliers and the passing on of skills acquired over generations. An industrial territory accumulates capabilities that live in people, plants, schools, organisations and productive relationships. How much of that remains available after a corporate transformation or a crisis requires specific local data.

Ariston's international growth, the sale of Indesit and the crisis of Antonio Merloni describe different forms of continuity and discontinuity. Their history does not authorise a single explanation of the present. It does let us question the difference between the success of firms born in a place and the capacity of that place to keep generating development.

## 19. Limits and contradictions

### The initial conditions of work

Sori documents that the first employees of the Albacina workshop were poorly paid and lacked insurance and union protection. To understand how working conditions evolved, we would need contracts, wage records and testimony from the period.

### Rural setting and industrial costs

Placing plants near where workers lived helped make manufacturing work accessible. Sori also reads the arrangement as a possible way of containing labour costs, thanks to the resources of the rural family economy. The interpretation is historically relevant, but it is not a direct measurement of wages or a personal statement by the founder.

### The concentration of decisions

The plants were spread across the territory and coordinated by a single company management. The local presence of the activities does not show a matching participation of workers in strategic decisions.

### The hypothesis of industrial paternalism

Paternalism is a category used in the history of industrial relations to describe forms of assistance and protection given by the employer together with strong authority over how work is organised. In the sources consulted directly there is no systematic documentation sufficient to describe the Merloni model in those terms. The category remains a possible object of research, to be tested against company records, workers' testimony and welfare initiatives actually attested.

### Public activity

The continuity of entrepreneurial initiative through successive offices in credit, administration and Parliament raises relevant questions about the relationship between economic power and representation. Responsibilities must be judged through concrete decisions, without presuming either conflicts of interest or a coincidence between private interests and the general interest.

### The results of the Foundation

The 1963 Foundation was an attempt to promote new firms beyond the original industrial core. Its early results were uneven, and Sori points to unsatisfactory or failed initiatives. Its later scientific and cultural continuity belongs to a separate phase of the institution's life.

### The absence of an organic theoretical system

We do not have a body of writings sufficient to attribute to Merloni a systematic political doctrine of enterprise and community. His experience is interpreted mainly through industrial and institutional practice. That distinguishes him from the explicitly formulated project of Adriano Olivetti, without lessening the historical interest of his action.

### The responsibilities of the successors

The corporate and industrial changes after 1970 belong to different generations, markets and decisions. The link to the founder concerns the inheritance of capabilities and firms, not a retroactive personal responsibility.

### The limits of the sources

The historical reconstruction depends heavily on the work of Ercole Sori. The 1924 project has not been consulted in the original; the question of the AGIP orders remains open; wage, trade-union and early Foundation archives have not been systematically examined. These limits bound the degree of certainty of the interpretations offered.

## 20. Details that illuminate the story

**A composite political formation.** In the young Merloni converged his father's socialist tradition, his mother's religiosity and a progressive Catholicism.

**An industrial project before the factory.** In 1924 he wrote for Buroni a document on the productive and economic possibilities of the weighing-instruments sector.

**A business started on limited means.** In 1930 he had about twelve thousand lire in savings and the backing of local financial relationships.

**A continuity with Pinerolo.** After the return to the Marche the new firm bought components and products from Buroni to resell under its own brand.

**A direct tie to farmland.** Between 1947 and 1949 Merloni bought six farms, reaching about 120 hectares of land.

**A diversification that began in mechanics.** Cylinder production came before the full expansion into household appliances.

**Four children.** Ester was born in 1922, Francesco in 1925, Antonio in 1926 and Vittorio in 1933.

**The Foundation came before the succession.** It was established in 1963, seven years before the founder's death.

**Economic research is a later legacy.** Since 1986 the Foundation has carried out a systematic analysis of the financial statements of the main Marche firms.

## 21. Common misconceptions

**Crediting Merloni with the birth of the whole Fabriano industry.** Fabriano already had important manufacturing traditions, above all in paper and mechanical engineering.

**Presenting Mattei as the founder of Merloni's diversification.** The 1953 meeting opened a new industrial opportunity, but Merloni already had a consolidated firm and productive skills of his own.

**Confusing Merloni and Olivetti.** Merloni's story is reconstructed mainly from entrepreneurial and institutional practice; Olivetti also worked out an explicit political and community project.

**Treating the fulfilment of the AGIP orders as definitively established.** The secondary sources give different accounts and the original commercial documents have not been consulted.

**Attributing to the founder every decision of the later firms.** The activities of Antonio, Vittorio and Francesco developed autonomously and belong to a longer industrial history.

**Identifying the Merloni legacy only with crisis.** Ariston Group preserves significant industrial and international continuity, while the Foundation has developed a scientific role in the region.

**Using Ariston's worldwide revenue as a measure of the economic value produced in Fabriano.** The consolidated accounts include international activities and must be distinguished from the actual territorial contribution.

**Attributing to Aristide the Foundation's later scientific initiatives.** A substantial part of its research activity began after his death.

## 22. Glossary

**Albacina:** a hamlet of the municipality of Fabriano where Aristide Merloni was born and where he started making scales in 1930.

**Inland areas (*aree interne*):** territories whose distance from essential services is now a criterion of Italian regional policy. The contemporary category does not automatically match twentieth-century administrative definitions.

**Platform scales and steelyards (*bascule e stadere*):** mechanical instruments used to measure weight.

**Risk capital:** resources invested in the ownership of a firm, exposed to the results of its activity.

**Productive diversification:** widening activity towards different products, processes or markets.

**District economy:** a territorial organisation of production founded on relations among firms, specialised skills and the local social setting.

**Family firm:** an economic activity in which ownership, management and succession are closely tied to a family.

**Diffused industrialisation:** the growth of manufacturing through production units spread over several places.

**Worker-peasant (*operaio-contadino*):** a manufacturing worker who keeps economic and social ties to family farming.

**Industrial paternalism:** a historical category for forms of employer assistance and protection accompanied by strong authority over work. Applying it to the Merloni case requires further evidence.

**Consolidated revenue:** a corporate group's revenue calculated to include the activities of its subsidiaries under the applicable accounting criteria.

**Reindustrialisation:** the process of rebuilding or replacing productive activity in a territory hit by industrial crisis.

**Productive specialisation:** concentrating a plant's activity on certain processes or product families.

**Territorial development:** economic and social transformation observable through work, income, skills, services, firms and institutions.

## 26. Frequently asked questions

**1. Who was Aristide Merloni?**

Aristide Merloni (1897–1970) was an entrepreneur from the Marche who founded the industrial business from which several companies of the Merloni family later emerged. He was mayor of Fabriano, a senator of the Italian Republic and founder of the Foundation that bears his name.

**2. Where was Aristide Merloni born?**

He was born on 24 October 1897 in Albacina, in the municipality of Fabriano.

**3. What was his educational background?**

He qualified as an industrial technician at the Montani Institute in Fermo in 1916 and gained his professional experience at the Buroni factory in Pinerolo.

**4. When did he start his industrial business?**

In 1930 he began making weighing instruments in a small workshop in Albacina. SAMA was established in 1933.

**5. What did the company initially produce?**

Scales, platform scales, steelyards and other mechanical weighing instruments. Production later diversified into LPG cylinders and household appliances.

**6. What was his relationship with Enrico Mattei?**

They met in Fabriano in 1953. Their meeting helped open an industrial opportunity that led to the construction of the Matelica plant in 1954.

**7. Was the AGIP order actually fulfilled?**

The published accounts differ. Sori describes a proposed order for 70,000 cylinders that never materialised; a later local memoir mentions two other orders. Without the original commercial records, it has not been possible to establish whether these refer to the same supplies or to different occasions.

**8. What was the Merloni model?**

An industrial organisation based on specialised plants spread across the territory and coordinated by a single management, closely related to the rural social structure of the Fabriano area.

**9. What does *worker-peasant* mean?**

A worker who combines manufacturing employment with continuing activities or resources connected to the family's rural economy.

**10. What were the working conditions of Merloni's first employees?**

According to Ercole Sori, the first workers in Albacina were poorly paid and lacked insurance and trade-union protection. By 1936, employees at the Fabriano plant were reportedly better paid than those in Albacina.

**11. Which public offices did Merloni hold?**

He chaired the Cassa di Risparmio di Fabriano from 1943 to 1951, served as mayor from 1951 to 1957 and represented Christian Democracy in the Senate from 1958 to 1970.

**12. When was the Aristide Merloni Foundation established?**

In 1963, to support new businesses and promote economic and social development in inland Marche.

**13. What does the Aristide Merloni Foundation do today?**

It works in economic research, culture and education. Since 1986 it has produced a ranking of leading Marche companies, in scientific collaboration with the Polytechnic University of Marche.

**14. Who were Aristide Merloni's children?**

Aristide and Maria Mattioli had four children: Ester, Francesco, Antonio and Vittorio. The three sons went on to hold different responsibilities in industry.

**15. What happened to Antonio Merloni SpA?**

The business independently developed by Antonio Merloni entered extraordinary administration in 2008. The crisis had serious consequences for employment and the territory, followed by sales procedures and attempts at reindustrialisation.

**16. When was Indesit sold?**

In 2014 Whirlpool acquired control of Indesit Company, the group developed by the industrial branch led by Vittorio Merloni.

**17. How are Indesit and Beko Europe connected?**

Beko Europe was formed in 2024 through an agreement between Whirlpool and Arçelik, bringing together European operations that had previously belonged to Whirlpool, including production historically linked to Indesit. Arçelik announced on 23 June 2026 that it had obtained full ownership through Beko B.V.

**18. What is Ariston Group today?**

It is an international industrial group specialising in thermal-comfort technologies, developed from the heating and water-heating business led by Francesco Merloni. In 2025 it reported approximately €2.7 billion in consolidated revenue; Paolo Merloni is its executive chairman.

**19. Does Ariston Group still operate in the Marche?**

Yes. It maintains manufacturing and technological activities in the area, including Albacina, Fabriano, Cerreto d'Esi and Arcevia. Assessing its local economic contribution requires specific evidence on employment, investment and actual production.

**20. Why does Aristide Merloni matter to Fabriano?**

He contributed to manufacturing growth, the spread of industrial employment in inland Marche and the changing relationship between enterprise, institutions and territory.

**21. Is today's industrial crisis a consequence of the Merloni model?**

The crises occurring decades after his death involve different companies, markets and decisions. Merloni's history nonetheless raises questions about how long productive capabilities can endure, and how vulnerable a place becomes when it relies heavily on a few industrial specialisations.

### Note on method

The reconstruction of Merloni's life from 1897 to 1970 relies chiefly on Ercole Sori's historical research. Institutional and corporate sources have been used for corroboration where available.

Interpretations of entrepreneurial motives, wage restraint and the distribution of territorial benefits are distinguished from directly documented facts. The 1924 industrial proposal is known through Sori's historical account. The original records of the AGIP orders have not been examined.

No systematic review of wage levels and industrial relations over the whole of Merloni's career has been possible. Developments after 1970 belong to his successors and to their respective economic circumstances.

Data on manufacturing change in Fabriano refer to the stated periods and territorial boundaries. Changes in business and population numbers have not been substituted for directly comparable employment data.

Information about the present reflects sources available up to 31 October 2026.

## 29. Closing — What remains

Aristide Merloni left the Marche to learn a trade and came home to build a business. The small workshop grew into an industrial organisation that helped transform a wide swathe of inland Italy. Skills acquired elsewhere became processes, factories and jobs for people living among the villages of the Apennines.

The change preserved some features of rural society while profoundly altering others. Nearby factories opened new ways of earning a living, and the company drew advantages from the economic and social conditions around it. Decisions about production, meanwhile, remained concentrated in a family business that went on to develop different activities and specialisms.

After the founder's death, the enterprises took separate paths. Some entered crisis; some changed ownership; others continued to grow in international markets. The Foundation established in 1963 also survived the generations, becoming a centre for economic research and documentation of the Marche's productive life.

These different outcomes resist a simple story of success or decline. Industrial capabilities may survive by changing form; they may also be lost when the conditions that allowed them to develop disappear. Their endurance depends on people, firms, institutions and opportunities to learn that have to be renewed over time.

History can show what Aristide Merloni helped to build. Today's transformations make a harder question visible: how much of that achievement became a lasting resource for the people and places where it began?

**What must a place be able to call its own if its future is to remain open when the companies that helped build it change?**
