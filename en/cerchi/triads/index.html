Triad 01

Truth, language, power.

Plato · Dante · Machiavelli

How can a city distinguish truth, give shared form to experience, and confront power as it actually works?

Truth must be learned

Plato makes education political: citizens must distinguish appearance, persuasion and knowledge.

Language gives crisis a form

Dante turns exile and civic fracture into an architecture capable of restoring orientation.

Power must be seen without masks

Machiavelli asks whether public language corresponds to the forces and decisions sustaining order.

PlatoDanteMachiavelli

Triad 02

Capital, values, desire.

Marx · Nietzsche · Leopardi

How does modernity organize what we produce, what we believe and what we desire?

What we produce can govern us

Marx shows how labor and social relations can acquire the appearance of autonomous economic forces.

What we inherit can outlive belief

Nietzsche shows how values can continue to command after their foundations lose credibility.

What we desire exceeds possession

Leopardi shows why multiplying satisfactions cannot answer desire whose horizon exceeds finite objects.

MarxNietzscheLeopardi

Triad 03

Time, form, disenchantment.

Seneca · Aristotle · Weber

Which forms can keep life, community and decision from dissolving into acceleration, apparatus and loss of purpose?

Time must be possessed

Seneca asks whether freedom is real when a person cannot govern the use of available life.

Power needs a form and an end

Aristotle distinguishes capacity from completion: what can be done still has to answer what it should become.

Organization can outlive meaning

Weber shows how rational systems can perfect procedures until means eclipse purpose.

SenecaAristotleWeber

Triad 04

Unconscious, hegemony, sovereignty.

Freud · Gramsci · Hobbes

Why do people obey — through inner conflict, cultural formation and the search for political protection?

The subject is already divided

Freud places desire and authority inside psychic structures that conscious self-description cannot fully command.

Normality has to be produced

Gramsci shows that institutions and cultural practices help shape the common sense within which power is judged.

Protection creates authorization

Hobbes shows why fear of disorder can make common authority appear necessary.

FreudGramsciHobbes

Triad 05

Interpretation, money, exchange.

Averroes · Simmel · Smith

What lets strangers share a world when meaning must be interpreted, value made comparable and exchange sustained by trust?

Meaning requires interpretation

Averroes shows that inherited texts and norms require methods capable of distinguishing kinds of discourse and claims to truth.

Complex societies need comparability

Simmel shows how abstract measures let strangers coordinate while placing qualitative differences on common scales.

Exchange requires trust before price

Smith shows that cooperation depends on justice, credible promises, institutions and moral judgment.

AverroesSimmelSmith

Triad 06

Republic, opinion, decision.

Cicero · Tocqueville · Condorcet

What makes political freedom durable: a common republic, civic capacity and procedures that turn judgment into revisable collective decision?

A republic needs something no faction owns

Cicero binds law, duty and public speech to an order that must survive temporary victory.

Liberty needs civic capacity

Tocqueville shows why rights weaken when citizens lose association, local action and dissent.

Decision needs formed judgment

Condorcet shows that counting choices cannot substitute for education, transparent procedure and revision.

CiceroTocquevilleCondorcet

Triad 07

Elite, mass, government.

Polybius · Mosca · Pareto

How do political form, organization and the circulation of ruling groups shape government without reducing politics to conspiracy?

Form limits power

Polybius asks whether institutions still contain real dependencies that prevent one part from becoming the whole.

Organization makes power durable

Mosca asks who possesses the continuity, competence and coordination to turn formal institutions into daily government.

Renewal decides whether power adapts

Pareto asks how ruling groups circulate, co-opt challengers and respond when their composition no longer matches social change.

PolybiusMoscaPareto

Triad 08

Who — and what — forms a people?

Manzoni · Collodi · Pasolini

Who teaches a people — and which environments make certain forms of judgment, desire and belonging possible?

Conditions of judgment

Manzoni shows how fear and institutional weakness expose the fragility of collective judgment.

Formation through experience

Collodi shows that a person is trained by incentives, temptations, work, care, deception and repeated encounters with reality.

Formation through environment

Pasolini shows how economic and media environments can reshape norms and desires without presenting themselves as education.

ManzoniCollodiPasolini

Triad 09

Desire, relation, judgment.

Epicurus · Gabriel Tarde · Daniel Kahneman

How much of our freedom is decided before we choose?

Desire sets the field before decision

Epicurus asks which desires answer genuine needs, which fears produce dependence, and how freedom changes when what moves us becomes more intelligible.

Read Epicurus ↗

Beliefs and desires have a social history

Tarde shifts attention from the isolated subject to relations: imitation, invention, opposition, conversation and publics help shape what later feels like one’s own belief or desire.

Read Tarde ↗

Judgment gives the problem a form

Kahneman shows how heuristics, anchors, framing and reference points can shape judgment under uncertainty before a person experiences the outcome as a deliberate choice.

Read Kahneman ↗

Triad 10

Matter, medium, information.

Lucretius · Marshall McLuhan · Claude Shannon

What changes when we distinguish what the world is made of, the media through which we encounter it, and what can be transmitted?

The real reaches the senses materially

Lucretius asks what bodies are made of and how images, movements and material processes can make the world perceptible without turning nature into a message addressed to us.

Read Lucretius ↗

A medium reorganizes the environment of experience

McLuhan asks what media do to perception, scale and relation when technologies become ordinary environments rather than visible tools.

Read McLuhan ↗

Transmission has formal limits

Shannon asks how much information can be represented and transmitted through a channel under constraints and noise without requiring a theory of meaning.

Read Shannon ↗

Triad 11

War, decision, and limits.

Thucydides · Carl Schmitt · Carl von Clausewitz

What happens to political order when war tests judgment, decision, and the limits of force?

War tests the city’s judgment

Thucydides shows how conflict enters civic language and decision, altering the conditions under which a city judges what is necessary.

Read Thucydides ↗

Exception puts authority in question

Schmitt asks who decides when ordinary rules fail and what happens when that decision claims to preserve or remake political order.

Force meets political purpose

Clausewitz examines how military force encounters political aims, friction, uncertainty and an opponent able to resist.

Triad 12

State, enterprise and community

Enrico Mattei · Aristide Merloni · Adriano Olivetti

Who builds the future — and who decides what it becomes?

Enrico MatteiEnergy, public enterprise and autonomyAristide MerloniEnterprise and the fate of placesAdriano OlivettiCommunity and power

In 1953, Enrico Mattei and Aristide Merloni met in Fabriano. Their conversation concerned an industrial opportunity. Mattei was seeking to expand the possibilities of Italy's public energy sector; Merloni had spent decades developing a manufacturing business in the inland Marche. Their interests met in the practical world of factories, markets and technical knowledge. The accounts of the proposed AGIP orders differ, and their respective ambitions should not be confused.

In Ivrea, Adriano Olivetti was pursuing another experiment. His company made internationally competitive machines, while his political thought addressed a question that industrial success could leave unresolved: what authority should belong to the people and communities whose lives are shaped by economic decisions?

These histories occupy three different positions in the organisation of industrial power. Mattei worked to enlarge a country's capacity to negotiate its economic dependencies. Merloni brought productive capabilities into places where employment had often required departure. Olivetti sought institutions through which workers and communities might exercise recognised powers of participation.

Their differences become especially significant when prosperity gives way to uncertainty. An enterprise may provide work without giving its employees authority over its strategic direction. Public ownership may expand national capabilities while leaving questions of political accountability unsettled. Social services may improve working lives without conferring control over investment or ownership.

The years after the founders' deaths make these tensions visible. ENI continued beyond Mattei. Merloni's industrial inheritance followed divergent paths. Olivetti's Management Council survived him until 1971, while his larger constitutional and ownership proposals remained unfulfilled.

What endures when a founder disappears, an employer changes ownership, or a technology loses its market?

The twelfth triad brings this question into the present. Industrial development can leave behind buildings, employment, skills and institutions. Their lasting significance also depends on what people and places remain capable of deciding when the conditions that first sustained them have changed.

Triad 13

Knowledge, consequences and decision

Edgar Morin · Rachel Carson · John Maynard Keynes

Question: Who can afford to wait when acting may cause lasting harm, but delaying a decision carries a cost of its own?

Morin examines the limits of knowledge and the relationships an explanation may overlook. Carson follows the consequences of intervention through living systems, often far beyond the original decision. Keynes shows how uncertainty can hold back investment and employment, leaving others to bear the cost of waiting.

Together, they open a civic question: who has the freedom to wait, and who must live with the consequences while others decide?

Triad 14

Law, Guilt, Punishment

Sophocles · Michel Foucault · Cesare Beccaria

A question to carry through the three guides: Who gets to decide when an act amounts to guilt, and how far may the right to punish extend?

SophoclesMichel FoucaultCesare Beccaria

These are distinct problems: respect for the dead, the administrative classification of risk, and the state’s power to punish. Their historical and legal differences matter. What connects them is the need to challenge a decision that claims to know everything that counts.
