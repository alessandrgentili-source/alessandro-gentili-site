Guide 21 · Circles of Ink

Vilfredo Pareto: elites, residues and circulation

Ruling groups can keep their positions after the qualities that once made them effective have begun to disappear.

Circles of Ink hubTriads

Introduction

Pareto enters the triad by asking what moves political and social action beneath the explanations people give for it — and how ruling groups change when their composition no longer matches the forces developing in society.

The Italian guide identifies a central fracture between self-description and effective capacity. People and institutions need reasons, doctrines and public justifications; those reasons can be sincere and consequential without fully explaining the forces behind conduct.

Pareto’s sociology is therefore useful only if its suspicion remains disciplined. The guide explicitly rejects the shortcut that every public reason is merely a disguise.

Logical and non-logical actions

Pareto compares the relation between means and ends as understood by the actor with the relation reconstructed by a logico-experimental observer.

“Non-logical” does not simply mean foolish or irrational. Ritual, loyalty, symbols and moral commitments can be socially ordered and effective even when their declared causal relation to an outcome is not what the observer identifies.

Residues: recurring elements beneath changing doctrines

Residues are analytical constructions derived by comparing actions and theories. They are not directly observed instincts or biological essences.

The source guide gives particular attention to the “instinct for combinations” and the “persistence of aggregates,” useful for analyzing the balance between innovation, negotiation, continuity and attachment within ruling groups.

Derivations: the public form of justification

Derivations are the arguments through which conduct is explained and made publicly persuasive: assertion, authority, appeal to shared principles and verbal proofs.

The guide insists that derivations are not necessarily conscious lies. Public reasons can create expectations, constrain institutions and be turned against the people who originally used them.

Governing and non-governing elites

Pareto uses “elite” as a positional category, not a moral compliment. High performers or powerful actors in different fields may belong to the broader elite; only part of it participates directly or indirectly in political command.

This widens the lens beyond formal office. Economic, cultural, scientific and organizational resources can prepare political power before they enter the government itself.

The circulation of elites

Circulation includes entry, exit, co-optation and replacement at the top. It is broader than electoral alternation: a government may change while administrative, economic or cultural selection channels remain largely the same.

The guide distinguishes movement of individuals from transformation of the mechanisms that produce a ruling group. A few new faces can renew appearance without opening access.

Democracy, representation and the problem of authoritarian appropriation

Pareto’s analysis shows minority rule inside representation, but the source guide also marks his normative limitations. Citizens, parties, unions and associations are more than audiences for elite competition; they can change the rules under which elites operate.

His relation to early fascism is treated chronologically: Pareto supported aspects of the initial fascist reaction and accepted appointments from Mussolini’s government, while retaining some liberal institutional limits and dying in 1923 before the completed dictatorship. The guide rejects both the claim that he was the regime’s full organic ideologue and any attempt to erase his real authoritarian affinities.

The contemporary wound: elites that govern after losing capacity

The source guide’s contemporary formulation uses “energy” as its own interpretive term, not a technical Pareto category. It names initiative, adaptability, decision and the ability to form new leaders.

Ruling systems can retain titles, networks and procedures while becoming unable to recognize new knowledge, demands and capacities outside their established circuits. Co-optation may correct this or merely absorb a few outsiders without changing the rules of access.

The democratic question Pareto does not answer is how to open real access, preserve competence and make replacement possible without leaving renewal to force alone.

Primary texts and orientation

Primary orientation follows the Cours d’économie politique, Les Systèmes socialistes, Manuale di economia politica, Trattato di sociologia generale, Compendio di sociologia generale, Fatti e teorie and Trasformazione della democrazia.

The Italian guide points readers toward Giovanni Busino, Fiorenzo Mornati, Norberto Bobbio, Luigino Bruni and Corrado Malandrino, and warns that residues and derivations can become circular explanatory devices if treated as hidden causes that always confirm the analyst.

The triad: elite, mass, government.

Read this guide as one node in the Polybius–Mosca–Pareto triad: three ways of asking how political form, organization and the circulation of ruling groups shape government without reducing politics to conspiracy.
